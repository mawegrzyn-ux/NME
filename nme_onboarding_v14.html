WRI International Technology · StoreConnect programme

New market entry — technology onboarding

Every Wingstop new market entry places technology on the critical path. The POS must be fiscally certified before the first transaction. The kitchen display system must be configured before the first order. The delivery platforms must be live before the first guest opens the app. None of these happen automatically — and none of them happen without the right people, the right vendors, and the right process in place.

This document is the end-to-end guide for how WRI International Technology runs the technology onboarding process for a new market. It covers the full six-month journey from initial market discovery through to first store opening — the stages, the gates, the vendor assessment process, how each component of the StoreConnect stack gets delivered, and what the brand partner needs to have in place to make it work.

It is designed for brand partners, their technology leads, and WRI programme teams. Use it to understand the sequence, the decisions that must be made and when, the risks that matter, and what good looks like at each stage.

WRI assessment process

Vendor assessment journey

From market discovery and pre-assessment through discovery and scoping, full ARB review, and tech stack sign-off — how WRI evaluates, selects, and validates vendors for each new market

Why we assess every vendor
🔐
SecurityGuest payment data, PII, and sales data flow through every vendor in the stack. A single weak link exposes the whole estate.
⚙️
CapabilityA vendor that looks right commercially may not be able to deliver technically. Assessment surfaces gaps before contracts are signed.
🛡️
Brand protectionA failed POS on opening night, a data breach, or a DSP outage on a busy Friday is a brand event — not a technology event. We assess to prevent it.
🤝
Partnership fitWe are entering a long-term operational relationship. A vendor's support model, escalation culture, and roadmap transparency matter as much as the product itself.
📈
Future-proofingThe stack needs to grow with the market — multi-store, loyalty, CRM, DSP expansion. Vendors who can't scale or won't integrate create debt from day one.
⚖️
ComplianceGDPR, local fiscal law, PCI-DSS, e-invoicing mandates. Non-compliant vendors create legal exposure for the brand partner and WRI — not just the vendor.
🔗
Integration confidenceIn a connected stack, every vendor must integrate cleanly. Assessment proves they can — before build starts, not after it.
💰
Cost of failureReplacing a vendor mid-deployment or post-go-live is an order of magnitude more expensive than getting it right upfront. Assessment is the cheapest insurance available.

What we are looking for in market discovery
🌍
Local vendor landscapeWhich technology vendors are active in the market — POS, payments, network, DSPs. Who is certified locally, who has support infrastructure, who has QSR/restaurant experience.
🍗
What other QSR operators useWhich POS, KDS, and payment platforms McDonald's, KFC, Burger King, and other major operators use in this market. A vendor already deployed in QSR at scale has a lower risk profile.
🤝
Brand partner existing experienceHas the BP used specific vendors in their existing business? Previous experience with Oracle, NCR, or a local POS vendor is relevant context — it does not determine the outcome but it informs the conversation.
🛡️
Our own stack availabilityIs Oracle Simphony certified and supported in this market? Are QSR Automations and QikServe available? Does Smarthotel have coverage? The StoreConnect path is always assessed first — local alternatives are a fallback.
⚖️
Fiscal and regulatory environmentE-invoicing standards, RT printer certification, tax reporting requirements, GDPR implementation. These affect which vendors can legally operate in the market and what additional components are needed.
📡
DSP platform landscapeWhich delivery platforms are dominant in the market — Deliveroo, Glovo, Wolt, JustEat, UberEats. QikServe's DSP relationships in this market determine whether the middleware approach is viable.

Pre-assessment — desk research filter

The pre-assessment screens vendors on the long list using publicly available information and WRI network intelligence — without approaching the vendor directly. Its purpose is to disqualify vendors early, saving the time cost of a full assessment for those that would clearly not pass ARB.

Pre-assessment screening signals
🚫
Automatic disqualifiersNo encryption at rest or in transit · Refuses DPSIA or ARB process · Wingstop data processed in AI/ML without opt-out · No ISO 27001 or equivalent · No formal incident response process · Single-market vendor with no support infrastructure · No evidence of QSR or hospitality deployment at scale
⚠️
Caution flags — proceed with scrutinyData residency outside EU/EEA without SCCs · Sub-processors not disclosed · No MFA on admin access · Incident response SLA not published · Less than 3 years in market · No public reference client list · Certification lapsed or pending renewal
✅
Passes pre-assessmentVendor clears the automatic disqualifiers and has no more than two caution flags. Progresses to full vendor assessment in Phase 4. WRI contacts vendor to initiate DPSIA and NDA.

Key risks
High
CRM decision deferred beyond UATIf no CRM platform is selected before UAT begins, integration testing cannot be completed. Loyalty cannot launch on any reasonable timeline.
Medium
Data residency conflictCRM must store customer data in a GDPR-compliant location. Some platforms have US-primary hosting that requires SCCs. Confirm in DPSIA.
Medium
Integration with POS not scoped in timePOS earn/burn integration requires Oracle Simphony API configuration. This must be scoped with Oracle during build — not added post-go-live.
Lower
HOF instance re-used without planIf BP uses the WRI HOF CRM instance for store 1, a clear migration plan to their own instance must be agreed before the second store opens.
Lower
Loyalty at kiosk not confirmedIf the kiosk UX does not include loyalty sign-up from day one, retrofitting it requires a QikServe change request and re-testing.
Decisions required
Braze, Salesforce, or local alternative?

Braze is recommended for new markets. Salesforce is available where BP has an existing relationship. Local alternatives require full RFP and ARB.

Owner: WRI Tech + BP  ·  By: Week 8
HOF instance for store 1?

If a WRI HOF CRM instance already exists and is compatible, BP can use it for the first store to avoid a full standalone implementation. Migration plan required for store 2.

Owner: WRI Tech  ·  By: Week 6
Loyalty at opening or 0–3 months post?

Earn/burn does not need to launch on day one. Customer data capture (sign-up) should start as early as possible. Split decision: sign-up at opening, full earn/burn post-opening.
